## 1. Product
AgeLens = interpretation + clarification assistant for age conditions in job ads, built around a job seeker's own background and specific question. Typical question: "I'm 38 with eight years of procurement experience. This ad says applicants should normally be under 35, but exceptions are possible. What should I clarify before applying?"
Show explicit conditions, exceptions and information gaps, combining the user's background with the ad's original text, to form evidence-based questions a human can answer. Audience: mid-career job seekers in ALL occupations (not only lawyers). It works by reducing information asymmetry and the cost of understanding conditions and preparing to ask. Employment improvement needs long-run validation; a small classroom trial cannot show lower unemployment or higher GDP. Distinctive feature = the whole flow: personal question → original-text evidence → uncertainty → clarification request → human reply. Compare Textio, Applied and one generic job-search product (service users, task, workflow) from verified official sources; a plain chat UI is not a technical innovation.

## 7. Job seeker & reviewer
Seeker flow: ask question → add background or upload CV → confirm → view original-text evidence → prepare clarifying questions → download brief or submit for human review. Question first in the form; background: occupation, age, experience, region, optional education. Drag/select PDF, DOCX, TXT CV; extraction editable and confirmed; parse failure → manual entry; never auto-send a full CV to an external model.
Results answer first: What does the ad explicitly say? What exceptions exist? How does it relate to my question? What can't be determined yet? What can I ask next? Keep original quotes, source id, data version; downloadable brief. Age is used to explain conditions, never to hide jobs, never to promise eligibility or inevitable rejection.
Reviewer: see requests, ad evidence and background the user consented to share; edit the reply; ask for more info; publish a human-confirmed answer; keep status history.
Empty results, duplicate submission, parse failure, timeout, no permission, expired session all get understandable messages and recovery paths. Ordinary UI shows no model parameters or internals.

## 10. Evaluation plan and tools
Main question: with the same background, ad evidence and interface, does LLM assistance raise the share of usable clarification requests versus a fixed template? Candidate design: participants randomised to two arms; each does four fictional tasks (age cap, qualification exception, age preference, incomplete evidence); identical info, time limit and UI across arms; analysis unit = participant (not each of the 4 answers). Two raters score independently: (1) accurate background; (2) faithful citation; (3) states uncertainty; (4) specific, answerable question. All four met and no severe unsupported claim → "usable". Define severe errors, calibrate raters, pre-specify disagreement handling. Also record time, model failures, missing data and dropouts. 24 participants is only a candidate: do power / minimum-detectable-effect analysis from baseline, meaningful effect and repeated-task structure; state assumptions and sensitivity; then set recruitment target, thresholds, stopping rule, assignment mechanism — for TEACHER APPROVAL. Freeze and pre-register before real collection. Implement task display, arm assignment, timing, answer saving, scoring and anonymous export. Formal tasks separate from public demo cases; classification accuracy ≠ workflow effect. Without a connected model and necessary approvals do NOT start a formal trial: only clearly labelled simulated sessions to test the system. Never fabricate participants or results. Report effect size, CI, sample size, dropouts and limitations incl. negative results. Employment outcomes need longer follow-up; macro effects = mechanism discussion only.
